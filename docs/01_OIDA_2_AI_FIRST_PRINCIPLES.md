# OIDA 2.0 AI-First Principles

1. **AI starts useful work.** Where generation or analysis is suitable, AI creates
   the first reviewable result instead of waiting to be used as chat.
2. **Authority is not automation.** Permission to prepare or execute never implies
   permission to approve, accept risk, or make a commercial commitment.
3. **Candidates do not silently become truth.** Every material output has an
   explicit candidate, committed, baseline, or approved status.
4. **Ground claims.** Important output includes sources, assumptions, gaps,
   confidence, and affected artifacts; hidden chain-of-thought is neither stored
   nor required.
5. **Use deterministic controls.** Authorization, validation, referential
   integrity, policy enforcement, and baseline immutability are not AI decisions.
6. **Spend human attention carefully.** Escalate exceptions, conflicts, material
   impact, low confidence, and authority gates—not every generated detail.
7. **Prefer reversible action.** Automated changes require audit and a practical
   undo or compensation path.
8. **Fail honestly.** Unavailable AI, incomplete grounding, invalid output, and
   external blockers are visible states; deterministic templates are not presented
   as AI results.
9. **Keep sources of truth singular.** OIDA references bounded-system truth and
   builds projections; it does not duplicate ownership casually.
10. **Prove one loop before expanding.** Capabilities exist to complete the Golden
    Flow, not to fill a registry.

## AI suitability test

For each action record the user problem, why AI is valuable, why rules alone are
insufficient, source context, expected output, human control, authority boundary,
and failure behavior. If rules are safer and sufficient, use rules.

## Explainability contract

The review surface shows source citations, explicit assumptions, unresolved gaps,
confidence band, alternatives when meaningful, and downstream impact. It never
claims to reveal internal reasoning.
